1. The "Too Big to Fail" Problem
By 1772, the EIC was in a massive financial hole. A few things went wrong:
* The Bengal Famine: Drastically reduced their revenue from India.
* The Smuggling Rivalry: Dutch tea was cheaper because it wasn't taxed as heavily, so everyone was buying "illicit" tea.
* Surplus: They had 18 million pounds of tea just sitting in London warehouses with no one to buy it.

2. The Tea Act of 1773 (The Bailout)
Parliament stepped in to save the EIC with the Tea Act. This wasn't a new tax, but a way to give the EIC a competitive edge:
* Direct Shipping: It allowed the EIC to export tea directly to the colonies, cutting out the "middlemen" (the British wholesalers).
* The Price Drop: This actually made legal tea cheaper than smuggled Dutch tea.
* The Catch: It kept the three-pence Townshend duty. Parliament figured the colonists would be so happy about the cheap tea they'd forget they were being taxed without representation. (Narrator: They did not).

3. Why the Colonists Hated It
It wasn't just about the money; it was the principle and the "monopoly vibes":
* Monopoly Power: The EIC picked specific consignees (colonial merchants) to sell the tea. If you weren't on the "approved" list, you were out of business.
* The Tax Trap: Accepting the tea meant accepting Parliament's right to tax them. Samuel Adams called it "the seeds of slavery."
